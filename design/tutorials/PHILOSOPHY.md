# Cause and Effect

Cause and Effect teaches each game as a visible exchange between a private phone and the shared TV. The phone shows the decision a player makes; the TV shows what that decision changes for the room. Every sentence must describe something that is clearly present in the current gameplay frame.

The stage is stable so the tutorial feels like one continuous demonstration rather than a stack of unrelated posters. Real interface captures crossfade inside truthful TV and phone frames. Short, game-specific overlays recreate actual controls only when movement is needed: Hues & Cues wheels scroll while the TV selector moves, Wavelength arrows move the public marker, and a Codenames reveal changes from the private Key tab to the flipped public card.

Words stay plain and operational. Each slide answers three questions: who acts, what they do, and what happens next. A headline, one short explanation, and two supporting rules are enough. Special cases such as Hitster steals, Mafia majority voting, Chameleon runoffs, and Wavelength sudden death receive their own visible beat instead of being buried in dense copy.

Motion exists to explain state change. There are no focus rings, animated fingertips, decorative pointers, or audio cues. The important movement happens inside the interface: a control changes, a card flips, a selector travels, or a score resolves. Slides dissolve gently while the stage stays in place. Pausing freezes both slide advancement and the internal demonstration so every keyframe can be inspected.

The sequence follows a real round: setup, private information, player action, shared result, scoring, and winning. The viewer can let the demonstration play, select an individual keyframe, replay the current sequence, swipe between slides, or use the stable Back and Next controls. Reduced-motion viewers receive the same complete sequence through immediate state changes.
