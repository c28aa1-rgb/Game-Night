# Lantern Ledger

Lantern Ledger is a visual philosophy of private decisions made visible only by their aftermath. Space is organised as a pool of warm light surrounded by smoke-black reserve: a single illuminated table, a ruled page, a face held at the edge of a shadow. It is meticulously crafted through measured asymmetry—one red rule just off centre, one card slightly raised, one name struck by hand—so attention lands where the story has changed.

Its materials are tactile rather than nostalgic. Tarnished brass catches an edge of light; oxblood is used as a consequence rather than decoration; paper carries graphite, thumb marks, and the quiet geometry of an account book. The palette is deliberately narrow and painstakingly calibrated, letting a player colour remain a practical recognition signal inside a world otherwise built from smoke, paper, brass, and red ink.

Composition proceeds like a table being prepared. A strong central axis holds the decision, while peripheral figures occupy the spill of the lamp. Deco brackets, ledger rules, and stacked cards create rhythm without ornamental clutter. Text is sparse and set as physical evidence: a theatrical title for the room, a literary sentence for the House, a typewritten label for a fact. Every alignment should feel like the product of deep expertise and patient typesetting.

Motion is weighted and intentional. Paper slides a short distance and settles; a flashbulb makes a verdict unavoidable; smoke takes longer than a decision to clear. The movement never performs for its own sake. It stages the primary action, gives a secondary material response, and keeps only a faint ambient life in the lamplight—master-level execution through restraint rather than noise.

The finished language should feel laboured over with painstaking attention: a collected artefact whose evidence is more dramatic than its explanation. It rewards looking twice, but never asks the viewer to decode decoration before they can understand what to do.
